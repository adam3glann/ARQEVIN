# ARQEVIN launch playbook

## Company description

**Short version**

ARQEVIN builds practical websites, business systems, and digital products, using AI where it solves a real problem.

**Expanded version**

ARQEVIN is a new independent software company in Cairo, Egypt. It helps businesses improve how they present, manage, and grow their work through websites, e-commerce, custom systems, backend development, integrations, and ongoing support. The company starts with the business need and chooses technology to fit the job.

Use the short version on social profiles. Use the expanded version in introductions and proposals. Do not describe ARQEVIN as a large agency or claim results the company has not measured.

## Launch checklist

### Brand and online presence

- Confirm the ARQEVIN name, logo, colors, and concise company description.
- Keep the website on its current `pages.dev` address until a custom domain is selected.
- Create and maintain company profiles on GitHub, LinkedIn, and Instagram; use matching descriptions and contact details.
- Add a business contact address and a working inquiry method to the site before promoting it. The current quote form is a preview and does not deliver submissions.
- Confirm the business's local registration, contracting, invoicing, and tax requirements with an appropriate local professional before accepting paid work.

### Portfolio

- Replace each case-study note only after checking the source project and confirming permission to show it.
- Keep Nuvanti's project relationship accurately labeled.
- Label PadelSync and Inventory & Sales Tracker as academic work.
- Use screenshots from the actual projects; identify any diagrams or concept visuals as illustrations.
- Ask for approval before naming a client or showing private data, admin screens, or unreleased work.

### Sales materials

- Use `PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.md` after an initial discovery call.
- Use `CLIENT_QUESTIONNAIRE.md` to collect requirements before estimating.
- Build a small project scope before setting a price. State assumptions, exclusions, timeline, payment milestones, client responsibilities, and third-party costs.
- Avoid publishing fixed package prices until delivery effort and the target market have been validated across real projects.
- Keep reusable starter architectures in `STARTER_KITS.md` and improve them from completed work.

## First-client goal: one to three paid projects

### Who to contact

Start with a small list of businesses where you can point to a concrete improvement:

- Local retailers and service businesses with outdated or hard-to-use websites.
- Instagram-first businesses that need a clearer catalog, inquiry flow, or store.
- Businesses using spreadsheets or repeated manual steps for booking, inventory, orders, or reporting.
- Introductions through family, friends, university, and professional networks.
- Carefully selected freelance-platform opportunities that match your current portfolio.

Do not send bulk or automated pitches. Contact people who plausibly have a need and personalize each note.

### Example first message

> Hi [Name], I came across [business] while looking at [specific detail]. I noticed [specific opportunity or friction]. I run ARQEVIN, a new software company building websites and practical business systems. If improving [specific outcome] is on your roadmap, I’d be glad to ask a few questions and suggest a small first step. Here’s our work: [website link]. No pressure if it’s not a priority right now.

Keep the first note short. Do not promise a result before understanding the business, and do not attach a proposal before the scope is clear.

### Qualify the lead

Ask:

1. What are you trying to improve or build?
2. How do you handle it today, and what is difficult about that?
3. Who will use the solution?
4. What must be included in the first release? What can wait?
5. Are there systems, data, payment providers, or vendors it needs to work with?
6. What is the desired timing and budget range?
7. Who will review the work and approve decisions?

Proceed when the problem is understandable, an owner can make decisions, and the expected scope fits your skills and available time. If not, narrow the scope or decline clearly.

### Propose and close

1. Send a written proposal with the problem, outcome, deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, timeline, milestones, price, and payment schedule.
2. Separate ARQEVIN's development fee from domains, hosting, payment-processing fees, email, AI usage, and other client-specific services.
3. Agree on the proposal and payment terms in writing before beginning work.
4. Request an initial payment before reserving time, then collect later payments at named milestones. Choose amounts based on the project's risk and scope; do not copy a percentage without considering the work.
5. Keep requested changes visible. Re-estimate changes that alter scope, timing, or cost and get agreement before doing them.

### Deliver and build trust

1. Share a short plan and confirm the person who can answer questions.
2. Demonstrate working increments on a staging link or screen share.
3. Test the agreed user flows, mobile layouts, accessibility basics, and relevant security needs.
4. Obtain client acceptance of the agreed scope and collect any final payment before handover, according to the written terms.
5. Hand over access securely, setup instructions, backups or exports where applicable, and a list of known limitations.
6. Offer maintenance as a separately scoped service.
7. After the client has used the work, ask whether they are comfortable providing a short, accurate testimonial. Request separate approval to publish their name, logo, and project details.

## A manageable weekly outreach rhythm

- Identify five businesses with a specific, observable opportunity.
- Send up to five individualized introductions; track the date, person, need, and next follow-up.
- Follow up once after a reasonable interval if there was no reply, then stop unless they respond.
- Spend time improving one relevant case study or demo rather than creating speculative portfolio claims.
- Review replies and adjust the offer based on actual conversations, not assumptions.

## Growth roadmap

1. **Portfolio and first clients:** finish verified case studies and learn to scope small projects.
2. **Repeatable delivery:** improve discovery, proposals, QA, handover, and starter kits.
3. **Maintenance relationships:** offer planned support to clients who need it.
4. **AI and automation:** add these where a measured workflow need justifies them.
5. **Custom business software:** deepen work in repeat operational problems.
6. **Product experiments:** validate recurring demand before building a SaaS product.
7. **Team expansion:** add collaborators only when sustained work supports the cost and coordination.

Revisit this plan after each few projects. Use evidence from delivery and client feedback to decide what to repeat.
